About Galacticio

A live map of the space around Earth

Galacticio shows every tracked satellite and debris fragment, the Moon, asteroids passing nearby, and the layers of air and radiation they move through. Everything is drawn to scale and moves in real time in your browser.

From the ground to 7.5 million km

The altitude scale is logarithmic, so the 12 km of weather and the 7.5 million km of asteroid space fit on one line. Bars show how many tracked objects sit at each height right now.

What you are looking at

Three kinds of things share the view. Each is handled a little differently, and the app labels which is which.

Tracked objects

Active satellites and catalogued debris from CelesTrak, the public mirror of US Space Force tracking.

    Moon and asteroids

      How it works

      Nothing is pre-rendered. The page downloads published orbital data once, then computes every position itself.

      Load the catalogue

      18,534 two-line element sets, each describing one orbit at a moment in time.

      Propagate

      The SGP4 model moves each object to the current second. Your browser does this thousands of times per second.

      Place the Moon and asteroids

      JPL Horizons vectors are interpolated to the current time, with the Sun direction for lighting.

      Stream the Earth

      NASA imagery loads in tiles as you zoom, from a 4,096 px globe up to 16,384 px (about 2.4 km per pixel).

      Live, snapshot or model

      Satellite motion is always computed live. The orbital data itself was downloaded on 15 November 2026 at 02:20 UTC. Element sets stay accurate to a few kilometres for several days, then drift, so the Galacticio server refreshes the catalogue from CelesTrak every two hours and space weather from NOAA every five minutes. If those sources cannot be reached, Galacticio falls back to a bundled snapshot and says so in the status pill. The Data sources page lists the state of every layer.

      Frequently asked questions

      Short answers, with the numbers Galacticio sees right now.

      How many satellites are orbiting Earth right now?
      Galacticio currently tracks 18,534 objects from the public CelesTrak catalogue. 15,853 are satellites, 10,608 of them Starlink, and 2,681 are catalogued debris fragments. 17,728 objects are in low Earth orbit, below 2,000 km.
      Where is the International Space Station now?
      The ISS flies about 426 km above Earth and circles the planet every 92.9 minutes at about 7.66 km/s, so it moves roughly 460 km every minute. Its live position is on the ISS page.
      How accurate are the positions?
      Positions are computed with the SGP4 model from published two-line element sets. They are typically within about 1 km when the elements are fresh and drift by a few kilometres per day after that. Good enough to see where things are, not for navigation or collision avoidance.
      Which asteroid comes closest to Earth in this window?
      2026 VU15 passes at 0.19 lunar distances on 12 November 2026. Galacticio shows all 41 known asteroids passing within 7.5 million km between 12 November 2026 and 15 January 2027.
      Where does space begin?
      By the most common convention, at the Kármán line, 100 km above sea level. The ISS and most satellites orbit in the thermosphere, between about 85 and 600 km, where the air is thin enough for orbits to last years.
      What is geostationary orbit?
      A circular orbit 35,786 km above the equator. A satellite there takes exactly one sidereal day to go around, so it stays over the same spot on Earth. Galacticio tracks 537 objects in or near that belt, mostly communications and weather satellites.
      What are the Van Allen belts?
      Two zones of charged particles trapped by Earth's magnetic field: an inner belt of energetic protons peaking near 3,000 km and an outer belt of electrons from about 13,000 to 60,000 km. The outer belt swells and drains with geomagnetic storms.
      Is Galacticio free? Do I need an account?
      Galacticio is free and needs no account. You can optionally sign in with Google to keep a watchlist of saved satellites and asteroids across devices.

      Cookie Policy

      Last updated 15 November 2026

      Galacticio uses no tracking, analytics or advertising cookies. It sets one cookie, and only if you sign in.

      The one cookie

      • galacticio_session keeps you signed in. It is set when you sign in with Google, lasts 30 days, cannot be read by scripts, and is removed when you sign out. It is strictly necessary for the sign-in you asked for, so it does not need separate consent.

      What Galacticio stores in your browser

      To remember how you like the view, Galacticio saves a few small settings in your browser's local storage. They are not cookies, never leave your device, and are not readable by other websites.

      • galacticio.imagery, galacticio.layer.*, galacticio.class.* — your view settings
      • galacticio.watchlist — objects you saved while signed out
      • galacticio.notice — that you dismissed the storage notice

      You can remove them at any time by clearing site data for Galacticio in your browser settings. Galacticio works the same without them; it just forgets your choices.

      Requests to other services

      Loading the page contacts a few services. Like any web request, these receive your IP address and basic browser information, under their own privacy policies:

      • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com) for the typeface
      • jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) for the three.js and satellite.js libraries
      • Google Identity Services (accounts.google.com), only when you open the sign-in menu. Google may set its own cookies there, under Google's cookie policy.

      Changes
