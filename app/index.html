About Asterio

A live map of the space around Earth

Asterio shows every tracked satellite and debris fragment, the Moon, asteroids passing nearby, and the layers of air and radiation they move through. Everything is drawn to scale and moves in real time in your browser.

From the ground to 7.5 million km

The altitude scale is logarithmic, so the 12 km of weather and the 7.5 million km of asteroid space fit on one line. Bars show how many tracked objects sit at each height right now.

What you are looking at

Three kinds of things share the view. Each is handled a little differently, and the app labels which is which.

Tracked objects

Active satellites and catalogued debris from CelesTrak, the public mirror of US Space Force tracking.

    Moon and asteroids

    Positions come from NASA JPL ephemerides. The asteroid list covers every known object passing within 0.05 AU (7.5 million km) between 12 November 2026 and 15 January 2027.

      Layers and fields

      Atmosphere layers use standard boundary heights. The Van Allen belts follow Earth's tilted magnetic dipole. The aurora oval is NOAA's OVATION forecast.

      How it works

      Nothing is pre-rendered. The page downloads published orbital data once, then computes every position itself.

      Load the catalogue

      About 18,500 two-line element sets, each describing one orbit at a moment in time.

      Propagate

      The SGP4 model moves each object to the current second. Your browser does this thousands of times per second.

      Place the Moon and asteroids

      JPL Horizons vectors are interpolated to the current time, with the Sun direction for lighting.

      Stream the Earth

      NASA imagery loads in tiles as you zoom, from a 4,096 px globe up to 16,384 px (about 2.4 km per pixel).

      Live, snapshot or model

      Satellite motion is always computed live. The orbital data itself was downloaded on 15 November 2026 at 02:20 UTC. Element sets stay accurate to a few kilometres for several days, then drift. When Asterio is opened from its own web address it refreshes the catalogue from CelesTrak and space weather from NOAA automatically. Inside claude.ai the page cannot reach outside sources, so it uses the bundled snapshot. The Data sources page lists the state of every layer.

      Cookie Policy

      Last updated 15 November 2026

      Asterio does not set any cookies. It has no analytics, advertising or tracking scripts, and it does not ask you to sign in.

      What Asterio stores on your device

      To remember how you like the view, Asterio saves a few small settings in your browser's local storage. They never leave your device and are not readable by other websites.

      • asterio.imagery — which Earth imagery you picked
      • asterio.layer.* — which layers are switched on
      • asterio.class.* — which object classes are shown
      • asterio.notice — that you dismissed the storage notice

      You can remove them at any time by clearing site data for this page in your browser settings. Asterio works the same without them; it just forgets your choices.

      Requests to other services

      Loading the page contacts a few services. Like any web request, these receive your IP address and basic browser information, under their own privacy policies:

      • Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com, fonts.gstatic.com) for the typeface
      • jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) for the three.js and satellite.js libraries
      • When opened from its own web address: CelesTrak (celestrak.org) and NOAA SWPC (services.swpc.noaa.gov) for fresh data
